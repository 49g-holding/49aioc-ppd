# History

**`[ORG]AIOC-PPD` v0.1.0 · 15 October 2026.**

## Why this stage exists

The Target Operating Model stage ends with a decided model: for each part of the organisation, its capabilities, its value streams, who decides what, its hand-offs, and a register of roles. It writes no process, procedure or policy. The stage after it was first framed as positions and their duties alone. That left nothing to say how the positions work together.

## How leading practice runs the stage after the operating model

A desk study of public material from large organisation-design practices, made on 15 October 2026 and checked by independent reviewers, found a common pattern:

1. Design principles, then the top two levels of structure.
2. A detailed design that cascades down, with structure, roles, decision rights, forums, interfaces and measures designed together; spans and layers serve as checks, not targets.
3. Roles are defined before anyone is placed in them.

Every practice studied warns that a structure drawn without its processes and decision rights fails. Most that state an order put the work first (processes, activities and decisions), then roles and decision rights, then structure; one puts structure first. In the classic process literature, jobs are built from the process. A common practitioner model of design levels keeps process and people work together down to high-level process maps, and splits them at detailed design. Job evaluation methods score written role profiles, so they come after them.

Standards used as references: a process classification framework with five levels and a process owner for each; process modelling with one lane per role and each step in one lane; supplier-input-process-output-customer documentation; decision notations with one accountable or one decider per decision; the quality-management requirement that each process has its responsibilities and authorities; and workload measured as volume times time per step against the time available.

## What this AIOC takes from it

- Processes and positions in one stage, processes first.
- Every position built from the steps and decisions it holds; job descriptions generated, never written by hand.
- Decision rights with one decider per decision; forums with charters; spans and layers as checks.
- Workload from volumes and step times.
- The design is walked through situations before it is decided.

## Why a separate AIOC, linked to the TOM

The governing seat directed that this stage is a part of its own, linked directly to the TOM, because the TOM release may have defects that only this stage finds, and the two must be able to go back and forth (`decisions/DR-0001`).
